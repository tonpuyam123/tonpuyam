Research

Molecular mechanisms of banana Fusarium wilt TR4 resistance, plant immunity, functional genomics, and host–pathogen interactions.

Current Research

My research focuses on the molecular basis of resistance to Fusarium wilt tropical race 4 in Cavendish banana. The global cultivation of Cavendish bananas is severely threatened by the soil-borne fungus Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense tropical race 4 (TR4). Because conventional breeding is limited by banana triploidy and sterility, resistant somaclonal variants provide an important route for understanding and improving disease resistance.

I investigate TR4-resistant somaclonal variants developed by the Taiwan Banana Research Institute (TBRI), with the aim of identifying causal somatic mutations and key regulatory genes. My work shows that highly resistant variants share common chromosomal deletions that reduce gene copy number and expression. Within these deleted regions, candidate susceptibility genes from the Autoinhibited Ca2+-ATPase (ACA) family were identified as important regulators of banana immunity.

Using a virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS) system, we demonstrated that silencing selected ACA genes in susceptible bananas elevates salicylic acid (SA) marker gene expression and reduces TR4 disease symptoms. These results suggest that reduced ACA expression enhances SA-mediated immunity and contributes to durable Fusarium wilt resistance.

Research Themes
Theme 01
Genetic basis of TR4 resistance in Cavendish banana

This work examines how somatic mutations, structural variation, and chromosomal deletions contribute to stable resistance in Cavendish banana somaclonal variants.

Theme 02
Susceptibility gene discovery and immune regulation

Candidate susceptibility genes, including members of the ACA gene family, are studied to understand how reduced expression can activate defense responses and limit disease progression.

Theme 03
Functional validation using VIGS and genome editing

Functional tools such as VIGS and CRISPR-based approaches are used to test whether candidate genes directly regulate TR4 resistance and can be applied in future banana improvement.

Ongoing Work
  • Development of CRISPR-edited banana lines targeting candidate ACA genes for stable modification.
  • Generation of transgenic Arabidopsis lines to investigate the subcellular localization and biological roles of candidate genes.
  • Functional testing of whether silencing or knockout of candidate genes can confer resistance in other susceptible banana cultivars.
  • Integration of genomics, transcriptomics, and molecular assays to identify disease-resistance mechanisms in crop plants.
Research Direction
Long-Term Research Goal
To understand how structural genomic variation and immune regulatory genes can be used to develop durable resistance against destructive crop diseases.
Focus areas include banana Fusarium wilt TR4, plant–microbe interactions, molecular breeding, functional genomics, and genome editing.
Research Areas
Molecular plant biology, plant pathology, functional genomics, genome editing, host–pathogen interactions, and disease-resistance breeding.
Model System
Cavendish banana somaclonal variants, TR4-resistant lines, susceptible banana cultivars, and experimental plant systems such as Arabidopsis.
Methods
Genomics, transcriptomics, virus-induced gene silencing, CRISPR genome editing, gene expression analysis, and disease assays.
